AI Agents vs. Standard LLMs: What They Are, What They Aren’t, and Why It Matters
===

Scott Wassmer

Published Mar 4, 2025


I spend a lot of time helping executives and senior managers understand how AI can fit into their organizations. One of the biggest misconceptions I come across is the assumption that AI agents and large language models (LLMs) like ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude are the same thing.

They aren’t. And that distinction matters.

Many of my clients come to me wanting to "build an AI agent" when, in reality, what they’re describing is either a basic chatbot or an LLM-powered assistant. Others assume that because an AI tool can answer questions, it must also be capable of independently executing tasks, which is where things start to break down.

So, let’s set the record straight. What exactly is an AI agent? What isn’t it? And how does it differ from a standard LLM like ChatGPT?

**An AI Agent Is More Than Just an LLM**

A large language model (LLM) is a very advanced predictive text generator—a system that processes input, predicts the most likely response, and then generates text accordingly. It doesn’t "think" the way we do, and it certainly doesn’t act on its own.

An AI agent, on the other hand, is an autonomous system designed to complete tasks by interacting with tools, APIs, and data sources. Think of it as an AI-driven employee that can plan, make decisions, and execute workflows, rather than just respond to prompts.

Here’s how I explain it to my clients:

**A standard LLM is like a well-read expert** - it gives great answers when asked the right questions but doesn’t take action on its own.

**An AI agent is like a proactive executive assistant** - it doesn’t just answer questions; it takes the initiative to accomplish tasks using whatever tools it has access to.

For example, if you ask ChatGPT to summarize a report, it will generate a summary based on what you provide. But an AI agent could retrieve the latest version of the report from your company’s database, analyze it, generate insights, and then automatically email those insights to your team.

That’s a massive difference.

**What an AI Agent Is (and Isn’t)**

I’ve had a lot of conversations where companies assume they already have "AI agents" because they’ve integrated ChatGPT into their workflow. But here’s the thing: just because something uses AI doesn’t mean it’s an agent.

**An AI Agent IS:**

**Autonomous** – It can make decisions and execute multi-step tasks with minimal human input.

**Action-Oriented** – It doesn’t just generate information; it takes steps to accomplish goals.

**Connected to External Tools** – It interacts with APIs, databases, or other software to complete tasks.

**Capable of Planning & Iteration** – It can adjust its actions based on outcomes and refine its approach.


**An AI Agent IS NOT:**

Just a Chatbot – Basic chatbots rely on predefined rules and don’t have real autonomy.

Only an LLM Wrapped in Code – A true agent isn’t just an LLM with API access; it has a structured framework for decision-making.

Human-Level Intelligence – AI agents follow logical sequences but don’t "think" creatively or independently like a human would.

I had a client recently who wanted to deploy an "AI agent" for customer support. After some discussion, we realized they really needed a smart chatbot, not a fully autonomous agent. Their goal was to enhance support responses, not execute independent actions, which meant a fine-tuned LLM integrated with their knowledge base was the right fit.

On the other hand, another client was struggling with lead qualification in their sales pipeline. In this case, a real AI agent made sense, it could monitor inbound leads, extract key details, cross-check them against CRM data, and assign follow-ups automatically.

The key is understanding when you need an agent versus when an LLM-powered assistant will do the job.

**Where AI Agents Are Headed**

AI agents are still evolving, but they’re already proving their value in areas like

    Automating repetitive business processes (e.g., pulling reports, updating CRM data)
    Managing multi-step workflows (e.g., onboarding new employees, handling approvals)
    Proactively assisting teams (e.g., monitoring systems and triggering actions based on predefined rules)

The big question I get is, “Will AI agents replace human employees?”

The short answer? No.

AI agents are assistants, not replacements. They excel at structured, repetitive, logic-driven tasks, but they lack creativity, strategic thinking, and human judgment. Instead of replacing jobs, they free up human employees to focus on high-value work that requires intuition, creativity, and complex decision-making.

**Choosing the Right AI for Your Needs**

If you’re considering implementing AI in your organization, the first step is clarity. Ask yourself:

    Do I need AI to answer questions and generate insights? - LLM
    Do I need AI to take action and complete tasks? - AI Agent
    Do I need AI to interact with multiple systems? - AI Agent with tool integration

Both LLMs and AI agents have their place—but understanding their differences ensures you invest in the right technology for the right use case.
